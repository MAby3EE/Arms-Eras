01 / 08First World War
United Kingdom1907

Lee–Enfield Mk III

A long-serving service rifle whose story begins before 1914.

History & source

Adopted in 1907, the Short Magazine Lee–Enfield Mk III entered service before the First World War. It served British and Commonwealth forces through both world wars and remained in use in many places after 1945.

02 / 08First World War
German Empire1918

MP 18

An early example of a new class of compact military firearm.

History & source

Developed near the end of the First World War, the Bergmann MP 18 entered German service in 1918. It is widely remembered as one of the first submachine guns used in combat, appearing during the war’s final year.

03 / 08Second World War
United States1936

M1 Garand

A landmark in the U.S. Army's transition to a semi-automatic service rifle.

History & source

Designed by John Garand at Springfield Armory and adopted by the U.S. Army in 1936, the M1 became its standard service rifle in the Second World War. It continued in U.S. service during the Korean War.

Read the historical overview ↗
04 / 08Second World War
Germany1944

StG 44

A late-war design often discussed in the history of modern service rifles.

History & source

Developed in Germany during the Second World War, the design appeared in service as the MP 43 and MP 44 before being standardized as the StG 44 in 1944. It saw combat late in the war and is often studied as an important step in the development of the modern assault rifle.

Read the historical overview ↗
05 / 08Cold War
Soviet Union1949

AK-47

A family of rifles whose global spread reflects the geopolitics of the Cold War.

History & source

Designed in the Soviet Union by Mikhail Kalashnikov in the late 1940s, the AK was adopted by Soviet forces in 1949. Production and distribution across many countries during the Cold War made it one of the most widely recognized firearm designs in modern history.

Read the historical overview ↗
06 / 08Cold War
United States1964

M16

A design that became closely associated with U.S. military history in Vietnam.

History & source

Developed from the ArmaLite AR-15 design, the M16 entered U.S. military service in 1964. Its adoption and early use during the Vietnam War made it a defining part of the history of American forces in that conflict; later versions continued in service for decades.

Read the historical overview ↗
07 / 08Late 20th century
Soviet Union1974

AK-74

A later development in the AK family, adopted during the Soviet era.

History & source

Introduced in 1974, the AK-74 was a Soviet-era successor within the Kalashnikov rifle family. It served Soviet forces during the late Cold War and remained in use across several countries after the Soviet Union dissolved.

Read the historical overview ↗
08 / 0821st century
Russian Federation2018

AK-12

A contemporary Russian service rifle, set within a changing military landscape.

History & source

Developed in the 2010s as a modern Russian service-rifle program, the AK-12 was adopted in 2018. Variants have since been documented in Russian service, including during the full-scale invasion of Ukraine; equipment reports should be dated because configurations have changed.
